# Chapter V — Final UAT evidence for researcher integration

## A. Summary of findings

Eight consenting, unique submissions supplied 47 five-point ratings each. All 376 item responses were in the top two categories: 168/168 navigation/usability, 72/72 feature helpfulness, 64/64 desktop/laptop, and 72/72 tablet/mobile. This describes eight people giving repeated ratings. Seven identified as beginners; seven performed the second device test on mobile phones and one on a tablet. The lowest mean was for manageable horizontal scrolling on tablet/mobile (4.375/5, five Agree and three Strongly Agree). Every Q35 optional issue field was blank.

## B. Conclusions

The respondents perceived PSE Pulse as usable and its tested features as helpful for stock-market learning. Desktop/laptop and tablet/mobile statements received favorable agreement throughout. All eight agreed or strongly agreed they would consider using the website as a learning starting point and recommend it to a beginner; these are self-reported intentions. Helpfulness and clarity ratings do not demonstrate measured learning gains, forecasting accuracy, objective speed, privacy compliance, or full functional correctness. The available pre-response research methodology documents an 80% stakeholder acceptance criterion, corresponding to at least seven favorable responses among eight participants. The final UAT ratings satisfy the numerical favorable-response component where applicable. However, formal overall UAT acceptance remains pending verification of the approved criterion's scope, unaided core-task completion, and unresolved Critical/High-severity defect status. Adviser approval timing and prespecified mapping of the revised questionnaire to the designated acceptance items were not independently established.

## C. Recommendations

1. Reduce horizontal scrolling and review chart/text readability on smaller screens; Q33_06 was the lowest-rated item despite favorable scores.
2. Add concise first-time guidance, tooltips, and plain-language terms. Three Q36 respondents raised terminology support and two requested a walkthrough or guided tour.
3. Review visual design and chart legends; two Q36 respondents suggested these.
4. Preserve visible data-source, freshness, forecast-limitation, and non-advisory explanations while verifying their correctness and coverage in separate technical checks.
5. Use the current Capstone 2 Chapter III RTM, replace manuscript Tables 72–77 and related October 1 prose using `UAT_MANUSCRIPT_REPLACEMENT_GUIDE.md`, and omit the obsolete principal-versus-Naive question. FR-10 means comparative model-performance evidence in the current RTM.

## D. Limitations

The sample is eight researcher-designated respondents and is not representative of all PSE users. Seven were beginners; tablet evidence is from one participant. The workbook does not independently establish recruitment procedure, per-response form version, exact device viewport, observed task success, timing, or actual learning. Responses are ordinal self-reports and repeated within person; no inferential significance claims are made. A blank optional issue answer is not proof of defect absence. The response dates precede and coincide with the final PDF export, and the workbook lacks a form-version field. The available pre-response methodology records an 80% (7-of-8) criterion, but its approved scope for the revised final form, observed unaided task completion, and unresolved Critical/High-severity defect status remain unverified; no overall PASS is assigned. Forecasting performance must be assessed separately with the frozen RMSE, MAE, MASE, R², and applicable model-comparison evidence.
